Guidelines for Computer Science Thesis Title
Introduction
A Computer Science Thesis, based on CMO No. 25, s. 2015, is a technical report on a
systematic investigation of a problem that can be solved using Computing. It may
include a solution, an approximate or partial solution, a scientific investigation, or
the development of results leading to the solution of the problem.
A computer science thesis must be anchored on Computer Science principles.
1. Group Composition
1.1. Each group must be composed of students under the same specialization (e.g.,
Software Engineering, Data Science, or Artificial Intelligence).
1.2. A group shall have a minimum of three (3) and a maximum of four (4)
members as mentioned during course orientation and reflected in syllabus.
1.3. The formation of groups must be finalized and approved by the Thesis Course
Adviser before title proposal submission.
2. Thesis Title Requirements
2.1. The thesis title must be concise and specific, consisting of no more than
sixteen (16) words.
2.2. The title must indicate the target algorithm, model, or technique to be used in
the study (e.g., “using Random Forest,” “through Genetic Algorithm,” “based on
Convolutional Neural Network”).
2.3. Titles must reflect the main theme and application domain of the research.

4. Dataset Requirements
4.1. For Data Science specialization, the study must use a dataset containing at
least 10,000 records to ensure statistical and analytical validity.
4.2. Software Engineering studies may also require datasets if modeling,
training, or testing of components is involved.
• The number of records per dataset depends on the scale of the study: small-
scale typically use 100–1,000 records for simple models like defect
prediction or effort estimation, while medium-scale uses 1,000–10,000
records to support more robust models and cross-project studies.
4.3. Artificial Intelligent studies, selecting an appropriate dataset size and
structure is essential to ensure meaningful experimentation and proper model
evaluation.
• AI research, an ideal dataset size ranges from 1,000 to 50,000 records,
which is sufficient to demonstrate key concepts in modeling, training, and
testing. The main goal is to show understanding of the process rather than
achieving the highest possible accuracy. It’s also important to split the
dataset properly (around 70% for training, 15% for validation, and 15% for
testing) and clearly document all preprocessing steps. See appendix B.
4.4. Datasets must be ethically sourced and compliant with data privacy laws
(e.g., Data Privacy Act of 2012).

5. Group Member Changes
5.1. Changes in group membership shall only be permitted on a case-to-case
basis and must be approved by both the Thesis Course Adviser.
5.2. Valid reasons for member changes include health issues, academic
withdrawal, or personal circumstances beyond control.
5.3. All approved changes must be documented formally.
6. Faculty Consent and Authorship Agreement
6.1. A faculty member may present a thesis title on behalf of a mentee group only
with the group’s written consent, accompanied by a signed agreement.
7. Title Presentation Guidelines
7.1. Each group shall be allotted 30 minutes for title presentation:
10 minutes Introduction, Presentation of major of
the project
20 minutes Paper Presentation
15-30 minutes Question and Answer
7.2. Presentations must include:
• Proposed Titles
• Objectives of the Study
• Scope and Limitations
• Possible Algorithm/s
• Target Beneficiary
8. Course Grade
8.1. Course grade of the students will be computed as follows:
Breakdown Percentage
Panel Members 60% (total grade of oral defense)
Course Adviser 30% (course requirements)
Project Adviser 10% (mentoring requirements)
9. Title Modification Policy
9.1. A group may change their thesis title once after approval, provided it
receives endorsement from both the thesis adviser and panel members.
9.2. Title change requests must include a written justification and updated title
documentation.
10. Roles and Responsibilities
Course Adviser
• Guides students in selecting and refining thesis topics.
• Ensures compliance with institutional thesis guidelines.
• Coordinates scheduling of title presentations and progress checks.

Thesis Adviser (Mentor)
• Provides technical and research guidance throughout the thesis
process.
• Reviews and endorses all outputs before submission to the panel for
defense.
Panel Members
• Evaluate the technical merit, feasibility, and academic contribution of
the study.
• Provide constructive feedback and recommendations during
presentations.
• Approve or disapprove title proposals and revisions.
11. Technical Adviser and Investor Consultations (Optional)
• Experienced Technology Project Developers as Speakers in
Workshops and Seminars (c/o IALAP)
• Personnel from Different Fields (e.g. IT, Business, Creatives) – c/o
IALAP

12. Final Notes
• All submissions and approvals must be properly documented.
• Groups and advisers are encouraged to maintain consistent
communication through formal channels (e.g., adviser meetings, thesis
forms and letters).
• Submission of scientific papers must include AI detection verification
while ensuring adherence to Pledge of Honor for MILES Learners which
upholds ethical research conduct and academic integrity standards
at all stages.
APPENDIX A
Machine Learning Algorithm (AI Major)
1. Supervised Learning
• Description: Models learn from labeled data to make
predictions.
• Algorithms / Techniques:
o Linear Regression, Logistic Regression
o Decision Trees, Random Forest
o Support Vector Machines (SVM)

o k-Nearest Neighbors (k-NN)
o Gradient Boosting (XGBoost, LightGBM)
2. Unsupervised Learning
• Description: Models find patterns or structure in unlabeled
data.
• Algorithms / Techniques:
o k-Means Clustering, Hierarchical Clustering
o DBSCAN
o Principal Component Analysis (PCA)
o Autoencoders
3. Semi-Supervised Learning
• Description: Uses a small amount of labeled data with a large
amount of unlabeled data.
• Algorithms / Techniques:
o Label Propagation / Label Spreading
o Semi-Supervised SVM
o Graph-Based Learning
4. Reinforcement Learning (RL)
• Description: Agents learn optimal actions through trial-and-
error interactions with an environment.
• Algorithms / Techniques:
o Q-Learning
o Deep Q-Networks (DQN)
o Policy Gradient Methods
o Actor-Critic Methods
5. Ensemble Learning
• Description: Combines multiple models to improve predictive
performance.
• Algorithms / Techniques:
o Bagging (Random Forest)
o Boosting (AdaBoost, Gradient Boosting)
o Stacking
6. Online / Incremental Learning
• Description: Models update continuously as new data arrives.
• Algorithms / Techniques:
o Stochastic Gradient Descent (SGD)
o Online Perceptron
o Incremental Decision Trees
7. Deep Learning (subset of ML)
• Description: Uses multi-layer neural networks to model complex
patterns in large datasets.
• Algorithms / Techniques:
o Convolutional Neural Networks (CNN) – for images
o Recurrent Neural Networks (RNN) and LSTM – for
sequential data

o  Transformers – for language and multimodal tasks
