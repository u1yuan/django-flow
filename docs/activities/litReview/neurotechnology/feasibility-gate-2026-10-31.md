# Neurotechnology title feasibility gate (secondary data only)

**Date:** 31 October 2026 (Asia/Manila)  
**Status:** No title cleared for a first title proposal. This is a pre-study feasibility decision, not an empirical finding or a literature review.  
**Track:** New neurotechnology concept; separate from the hydroponics and diesel work.

## Decision and evidence boundary

The group confirmed that it has **no EEG or fNIRS equipment, recordings, AI-use logs, participant route, approval path, budget, or deadline**. It intends to use data already generated by a company, client, or open source. The repository contains no identified neurotechnology dataset. A public EEG dataset relevant to AI-assisted writing was identified, but its published fields do not establish the exposure measures or the subsequent matched task required by the shortlisted titles. No suitable linked open dataset was identified in this bounded search; this is **not** a claim that none exists. No raw signal was downloaded or analyzed, and no client access was represented as secured.

**Gate rule.** `Pass` means the prerequisite is evidenced for the named data source and study design. `Conditional` means a specific check or agreement could resolve it. `Fail` means the currently identified data or confirmed access cannot support the title as written. A title proceeds to the proposal only when all essential gates pass; a condition is not an approval.

| Gate | Evidence required to pass for a secondary-data study |
| --- | --- |
| Technical | Accessible raw or sufficiently documented EEG/fNIRS signals; usable channels/locations, sampling, timestamps, preprocessing and quality information; a feasible target measure. |
| Data | The **same participants** have repeated, time-aligned neural measurements and pre-target AI exposure records. For the original next-block titles, the source must identify a later, matched, AI-free target block and its baseline. |
| Participant | A source records participant IDs and enough independent participants/blocks for the intended evaluation; no new recruitment is assumed. Repeated windows from one person are not independent participants. |
| Ethics and reuse | Dataset license or client agreement permits this analysis, and the university confirms its required review, exemption, or approval route for secondary human data. A public license alone does not settle the school's requirement. |
| Operational | The group can obtain a data dictionary, protocol, access method, stable files, and any needed client clarification, then reproduce preprocessing and audit missingness and exclusions. |
| Cost | Acquisition, storage, compute, software, and any client or access charges fit a **confirmed** budget. No cost estimate is asserted before data size and terms are known. |
| Schedule | Access, quality audit, modeling, independent evaluation, writing, and school review fit a **confirmed** deadline. |
| Academic | The outcome, exposure, forecast horizon, unit, baseline, validation, and system contribution are defined and supported by the data; the RRL/RRS and gap are verified from opened sources. Forecasting skill is compared with a simple baseline, without turning association into causation. |

## Exact route to a pass

1. **Name one specific secondary dataset or client export.** Supply its owner, access terms, data dictionary, protocol, participant IDs, task labels, recording files, event or interaction logs, time zone/clock synchronization, and permission to use and report it. Do not deposit restricted client records in this repository.
2. **Check the exposure–outcome join.** For each participant and source block, verify that duration, frequency, reliance, or last-consultation time can be measured **before** the target. Confirm the subsequent target block and baseline where the title claims one. Do not infer individual AI consultations from an AI-condition marker.
3. **Audit analysis feasibility.** Count independent participants, sessions and eligible blocks; inspect channel locations, signal artifacts, missingness, clock drift, target coverage, and exclusions. Predefine the unit as participant × source block × target block for next-block titles. Use participant-aware train/test splitting and a simple persistence or subject-history baseline. Choose the forecast horizon only after the recorded block structure is verified.
4. **Clear institutional and practical gates.** Document the university's secondary-data ethics route, access agreement, budget, deadline, storage, and who can clarify the source protocol. Then select the narrowest title its fields genuinely support.
5. **Only after all gates pass**, draft the first title proposal with the course template's area, background/rationale, objectives, scope/limitations, beneficiaries, SDG category if supportable, references, and an AI interaction log. Build RRL/RRS and a comparison table from opened primary or official sources; state a specific testable gap without asserting novelty from this screen.

**Interpretation limits:** EEG band power and fNIRS HbO/HbR are indirect measurement proxies, not readings of individual neuron activation; see [EEG frequency-analysis guidance](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9717489/) and the [fNIRS trial's measurement description](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12255134/). A predictive association would not show that AI use causes a neural change. This document makes no claim of model performance, sample adequacy, or observed effects.
